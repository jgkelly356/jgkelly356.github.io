BAMA-2 Drag Sail Payload

Project Overview

BAMA-2 is a CubeSat mission developed by UASpace at the University of Alabama as part of NASA's ELaNa program. The drag-sail payload is a deployable system intended to increase atmospheric drag at end of life. We are working toward a deployment off the ISS in Q4 of 2026.

I serve as Payload Team Lead, managing a 7-person team responsible for design, testing, manufacturing, and integration of the deployment mechanism. My focus has been on integration and deployment reliability late in the design cycle.

Design Challenges & Solutions

Uncontrolled Boom Deployment

Problem: The initial deployment design released the booms via burn-wire, but friction with the surrounding cage frequently prevented full deployment.

Constraints: There was insufficient volume, budget, or schedule margin to add an additional motor or new active boom-control mechanism.

Solution: I designed a spring-loaded locking pin mechanism, released by burn-wire, to mechanically couple the inner and outer shafts.

Result: Boom deployment succeeded in every post-redesign test. A commanded motor bump reliably completes deployment.

Sail Stowage Sensitivity

Problem: The original sail storage volume required near-perfect folding, increasing the risk of failure.

Constraints: No allowance for additional mechanisms or major architectural changes.

Solution: I designed a longer sail mount and drive shaft, increasing available storage volume.

Result: Sail stowage became more repeatable and forgiving, reducing integration risk.
